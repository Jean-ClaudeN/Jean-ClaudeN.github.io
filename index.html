01 — About

Research, communication,
and human connection.

I am a Graduate Research Assistant in the Department of Biological Systems Engineering at the University of Nebraska–Lincoln, where my work sits at the intersection of agricultural systems, digital technology, and human behavior. I focus specifically on how farmers perceive, evaluate, and decide whether to adopt AI-enabled agricultural tools — a question that is as much about trust and communication as it is about technology.

Alongside my academic work, I have built experience across youth entrepreneurship, international agricultural communication, and digital strategy — supporting organizations including Generation Unlimited, FAO Rwanda, and Agriterra. I co-founded Agirite, a youth-led platform connecting rural farmers to knowledge, markets, and opportunity.

I believe that responsible innovation in agriculture requires listening to farmers as carefully as it requires building better tools. That conviction shapes everything I do.

03 — Research

Current academic
focus.

Master's Research

Farmer Trust and Responsible AI Adoption in Nebraska Crop Systems

My current research examines how farmers in Nebraska maize and soybean systems perceive and evaluate AI-powered recommendation tools. The study investigates the roles of perceived value, usability, data transparency, accuracy, and risk on farmers' willingness to adopt these tools — with the goal of producing actionable insights for developers, extension specialists, and policymakers who want AI integration to be both effective and responsible.

The work is grounded in social science methods including structured surveys and qualitative interviews, and is being conducted under the guidance of faculty at the Department of Biological Systems Engineering, University of Nebraska–Lincoln.
